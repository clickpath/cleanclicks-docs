How CleanClicks handles your data, your visitors' data, and privacy compliance.

## First-Party Data Architecture

CleanClicks operates as a first-party data processor. All tracking data flows through your own subdomain (`cleanclicks.yourdomain.com`), which means:

- **No third-party cookies.** CleanClicks uses first-party cookies only.
- **No cross-site tracking.** Data is scoped to your domain.
- **No data resale.** Your conversion data is never shared with other CleanClicks customers, sold, or used for any purpose other than relaying it to the ad platforms and analytics tools you connect.

CleanClicks also provisions a dedicated GA4 property that it owns and operates on your behalf under service-provider terms. It is used for first-party, server-side measurement and is separate from your own primary GA4 property, which your existing analytics stack continues to populate untouched.

By default, CleanClicks measures visitors in the United States only (a US-only geo allowlist). You can add other countries or turn geo filtering off in Configuration > Traffic Filters.

## What Data Is Collected

### From Visitors

| Data | Purpose | Storage |
|---|---|---|
| Session ID | Track visitor across pages | Your site's own browser storage, not a cookie; kept until the browser tab is closed |
| Click IDs (gclid, fbclid, etc.) | Ad attribution | Your site's own browser storage, not a cookie. On pages that load CleanClicks's own script, each is checked against a 90-day limit, and a record of the latest of each kind is kept with no time limit. The WordPress plugin also keeps its own copy, for as long as its installed version decides; a Shopify pixel keeps its copy with no time limit |
| UTM parameters | Campaign attribution | Your site's own browser storage, not a cookie, with no time limit. CleanClicks's own script and the WordPress plugin replace them when a later visit brings campaign details of its own, with some exceptions; a Shopify pixel updates them and keeps the first landing page |
| IP address | Geo classification + bot detection | Used in real time; kept in conversion records and traffic analytics for up to 90 days, and in security audit logs for up to 180 days |
| User agent | Device classification, bot detection | Used in real time; kept in conversion records for up to 90 days, and in security audit logs for up to 180 days |
| Page URL | Trigger matching | Kept with the conversion record for 90 days |
| Email (when provided) | Cross-platform matching | SHA-256 hashed in the browser, and sent by CleanClicks's browser code only as that hash (see the note below); on CleanClicks's servers the hash is kept in the email-to-click-ID map for 90 days, counted again from each update |

In the browser, the email hash is also kept in your site's own storage. On pages that load CleanClicks's own script, it is used for no more than 90 days after it was stored. The WordPress plugin keeps its own copy for as long as its installed version decides, and a Shopify pixel keeps it with no time limit. Items with no time limit stay until the visitor clears your site's data in their browser.

CleanClicks's browser code does send the address of the page the visitor is on, the page they came from, and the page they first landed on, as they are. If your site puts an email address into a page address, it reaches CleanClicks inside that address; CleanClicks removes it before it keeps or passes on that address, except inside an advertising click-identifier value, where it can remain.

### From Ecommerce

| Data | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Order ID | Deduplication |
| Order total / currency | Revenue reporting |
| Order tax and shipping amounts | Purchase reporting in Google Analytics |
| Coupon codes applied to a cart or an order (WooCommerce) | Purchase reporting in Google Analytics |
| Product details | Product-level conversion tracking |
| Customer email | Hashed for cross-platform matching |

On a site with Google Analytics connected, and for a visitor who has not opted out, tax and shipping go to Google Analytics with the purchase event, and coupon codes with the checkout and purchase events. For a visitor who has opted out, CleanClicks still stores them, and whether they reach Google Analytics depends on the path. From CleanClicks's own conversion sending, an opted-out visitor's conversion goes to Google Analytics only if CleanClicks has set a measurement-only override for Google Analytics on your site's record; that is not a setting in your dashboard. Separately, where your site relays Google Analytics through your CleanClicks subdomain, an opted-out visitor's purchase can still reach Google Analytics in a stripped form that keeps the tax and shipping amounts and carries no coupon code. That stripped feed is on by default. It sends nothing for a request that carries a Global Privacy Control signal, and only CleanClicks can switch it off for your site. Of the events CleanClicks's servers build and send themselves, only those to Google Analytics carry them. An analytics tool your site relays through your CleanClicks subdomain can receive what that tool's own script sends, which can include them. Where CleanClicks receives them in a conversion message, it stores them with the conversion record for 90 days: that is the case for the WooCommerce plugin, and for the Shopify Custom Pixel from version 2.8.0. With an earlier pixel version, tax and shipping can still reach Google Analytics through your CleanClicks subdomain, and CleanClicks does not store them. Of the CleanClicks integrations, only the WooCommerce plugin sends coupon codes.

On a WooCommerce site with Usermaven switched on, the plugin can also send events from your own server straight to Usermaven, on two paths. Cart and checkout-start events carry the coupon codes applied to the cart (product-view events carry none), and the checkout start also carries the cart's tax, shipping and discount totals. This path stops for a request that shows an opt-out (a Global Privacy Control signal, or one of your site's opt-out cookies) or a refusal of the CleanClicks service by name. Where CleanClicks treats consent as required for your site (by default once the plugin has reported the WP Consent API to it) or the plugin's Require Consent setting is on, it also stops where the WP Consent API reports that consent was refused or, with that setting on, has not been given. Where CleanClicks treats consent as required and the WP Consent API is not loaded, this path sends nothing. This path can miss an opt-out made on the CleanClicks privacy page, and it does not read a refusal recorded only against an email address. Routing orders through CleanClicks does not stop it. Order events carry the order's totals for tax, shipping and discounts, the payment method, the billing and shipping state and country, and, for a new order, a completed order and an import of past orders, the coupons used. This second path stops where CleanClicks has routed your site's orders through its own servers, which is a setting CleanClicks makes on your site's record and not one in your dashboard, and the plugin can read that setting when it sends the order, or when the order records a refusal.

These are the main order fields. An order message from your store's server can contain more than this table lists.

## Data Retention

| Data Type | Retention Period |
|---|---|
| Conversion events | 90 days |
| Email-to-click-ID mapping | 90 days, counted again from each update; a mapping that holds only an opt-out flag, up to 25 months |
| Opt-out records | Up to 25 months, or 72 hours for some refusals; kept through a deletion request |
| Failed event retries | 90 days |
| Traffic analytics | 90 days |
| Conversion audit log | 180 days, except the record of a deletion request that was run and the record of a request to lift an opt-out, which are kept with no expiry |
| Visitor ID cookie (cc_pid) | 1 year, set again for another year by each conversion event that carries it and no opt-out signal |
| Click IDs, campaign details, session ID, visit count and email hash in your site's browser storage | As described above; the visit count, and some of the others, have no time limit |
| Session cookie (cc_sid) | 30 minutes |
| Deduplication records | Up to 7 days (24 hours by default, 48 hours for order webhooks) |

After the retention period, data is automatically purged. Some records are kept longer on purpose: opt-out records are kept through a deletion request, so that the opt-out stays in force, and the record of a deletion request that was run and the record of a request to lift an opt-out are kept with no expiry, as evidence. ## Hashing and Encryption

- **Customer emails** are SHA-256 hashed before being sent to any ad platform. In what CleanClicks's servers build and send, only the SHA-256 hash is passed on, apart from an email address inside an advertising click-identifier value. The first-party analytics proxy does not screen the page addresses in your site's own Google tag requests, so an email address your site puts into a page address can reach Google that way.
- **All stored data** is encrypted at rest. The Google Analytics refresh token is encrypted at rest using AES-256 with a per-account key.

## CCPA and opt-out behavior

When a visitor opts out (via Global Privacy Control, a CleanClicks opt-out cookie, a refusal recorded against the visitor's hashed email address, or your consent platform's "reject" applied through the WordPress plugin's consent bridge), CleanClicks does not transmit to advertising platforms a conversion from a request or order it can tie to that opt-out. For such a request or order:

- **Meta, TikTok, and Microsoft Advertising receive nothing.**
- **Google Ads.** No identifying conversion is sent. Advertising click identifiers are removed, and the server-side conversion upload is not made. On pages that load CleanClicks's own script, that script sets Google's consent signals for ad storage, ad user data, and ad personalization to denied for a browser that sends a Global Privacy Control signal or carries the opt-out cookie, unless your site's own cookie banner changes those settings. CleanClicks's Shopify pixel and the theme code CleanClicks provides for Shopify do not load that script, so on a Shopify store that uses only those, no CleanClicks code sets these signals.
- **Google Analytics, through your site's first-party analytics proxy.** For a request that carries a Global Privacy Control signal, the proxy forwards no page visit, event, or purchase to Google Analytics. For another opt-out the proxy can see on the request, the proxy by default still forwards page visits, events, and purchases to Google Analytics with identifying details removed: no IP address, no user or visitor identifier, no advertising click identifier, no page title, and no session identifier; a new random client identifier is used on every hit; and ads personalization is marked off. CleanClicks can switch this feed off for your site.
- **Analytics events CleanClicks's servers build and send (Google Analytics Measurement Protocol and Usermaven).** Nothing is sent, unless a measurement-only setting has been switched on for that destination for your site and the visitor's refusal is known not to cover analytics.
- **Personal identifiers are stripped** where such a setting is on: the hashed email address, IP address, user agent, location, device type, campaign details (other than campaign tags in the page address), and advertising click identifiers are removed.

For the limits on a Shopify store, see the CleanClicks privacy policy (Section 4).

CleanClicks does not transmit Limited Data Use or limited-data-use flags. The previous LDU pathway was removed on 2026-05-29 so that opted-out visitors are dropped before dispatch rather than sent under a restriction flag.

## GPC (Global Privacy Control)

CleanClicks acts on GPC for each request that carries it. On pages that load CleanClicks's own script, GPC is detected at both the browser and server level. The tracking script reads the browser's `navigator.globalPrivacyControl` signal, and the CleanClicks edge worker also honors the `Sec-GPC` request header and the CleanClicks opt-out cookie directly. A GPC signal is treated as a valid opt-out under California law. CleanClicks's Shopify pixel does not read the signal in the browser; whether the pixel's requests carry it depends on the browser and on Shopify. On WordPress, the plugin additionally integrates with the WP Consent API to detect consent-management platforms.

## GDPR

CleanClicks markets the service exclusively to businesses in the United States and does not direct it to data subjects in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland. For EU-focused businesses, consult your legal team about compliance requirements with the current feature set.

## Data Residency

Conversion data is processed globally across CleanClicks infrastructure. If your business requires data to remain in a specific geographic region, contact [helpdesk@cleanclicks.io](mailto:helpdesk@cleanclicks.io) to discuss options.

## Your Obligations

As a CleanClicks customer, you are responsible for:

- **Disclosing tracking in your privacy policy.** Your site's privacy policy should mention the use of first-party tracking technology for conversion measurement.
- **Respecting visitor consent.** If your jurisdiction requires consent for tracking (GDPR, ePrivacy), implement a consent mechanism on your site.
- **Managing ad platform compliance.** Each ad platform has its own data processing terms. Ensure your use of those platforms complies with their policies.

## Accessing or Deleting Your Data

To request data export or deletion, contact [helpdesk@cleanclicks.io](mailto:helpdesk@cleanclicks.io). Account data (profile, configuration) can be exported or deleted on request.

For visitor data: most visitor records on CleanClicks's servers expire on their own, on the schedule in the Data Retention table above. Opt-out records are kept through a deletion request, and the records of a deletion request and of a request to lift an opt-out are kept with no expiry. For urgent deletion requests, contact support.
